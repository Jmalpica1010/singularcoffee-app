SingularCoffee Privacy Policy

Last updated: 3 August 2026

SingularCoffee is an Android app for brewing specialty coffee. This policy describes what data it handles, where that data lives, and the cases in which it leaves your phone.

It was written by reading the code, not from memory. Where it says something is not sent, it is because the code that would send it does not exist.

Summary

  • No accounts, no sign-up, no login. The app does not know who you are.
  • No analytics, telemetry, advertising or automatic crash reporting. No third-party SDK that collects usage data is included.
  • Everything you record is stored on the phone itself, in a database private to the app.
  • There is no server of ours, so there is nothing to upload to one.
  • Data leaves the phone in three situations you start yourself and in one automatic one belonging to the operating system, which you can turn off. All four are detailed below.

2. What the app stores

You write all of this and it stays on the phone. It never asks for your name, your email, your phone number or your location. The only identifying field is the Instagram username, and it is optional: the app works the same without it.

Coffees. Name, roaster, country, region, farm, producer, varietals, process and its detail, altitude, roast level and date, declared score, roaster's notes, price per kilo, bag size, remaining grams, and a free notes field.

Equipment. Brewers, grinders (burr type and diameter, adjustment range and step), accessories and water profiles (hardness, alkalinity, calcium, magnesium, sodium, TDS and pH).

Brews. Date and time, dose, target water, grind, temperature, number of pours, final beverage weight, total time, drawdown time, TDS if you enter it, dry spent grounds weight if you measure it, the ten-attribute SCA cupping form, the flavour descriptors you pick or type, your tasting notes, a rating, and the recipe used with its author.

Scale curve. Every recorded brew keeps the series of weights with their timestamps — what draws the curve — and the pours detected from it.

Recipes. The ones you create and the ones you import from the public catalogue.

Preferences. Language, weight unit, temperature scale, whether the TDS field is shown, and whether recipes sync.

Your Instagram username, if you choose to enter one. It is optional, stored on the phone, and there is no login: the app does not connect to Instagram, does not verify that the account is yours, and does not access anything in it. Its only use is to sign the images you generate, so whoever sees them can find you. You can remove it at any time from the same place, and images generated afterwards stop carrying it.

Label photographs. If you use label scanning, the image is stored temporarily in the app's private cache and is overwritten on the next scan. It is not saved to your gallery and is not attached to the coffee: all that is kept from the photo is the text you confirm.

What the app never stores

Your real name, your email, your phone number, your contacts, your location, your IP address, advertising identifiers, the device identifier, or anything about other apps. The Instagram username is the one exception, it is optional, and you type it yourself.

3. Permissions and what they are for

Permission What for Required
Bluetooth (BLUETOOTH_SCAN, BLUETOOTH_CONNECT) Find your scale and read weight, flow rate and timer. No: without a scale the app works with its own timer.
Precise location (ACCESS_FINE_LOCATION) Android 11 and earlier only. Those versions require the location permission in order to scan for Bluetooth devices. No
Camera Photograph the bag's label to read its text. No
Internet Download the public recipe catalogue. No
Storage (WRITE_EXTERNAL_STORAGE) Android 9 and earlier only. Save the image you generate for sharing to your gallery. No

About the location permission, which is the one that usually worries people: scanning is declared with neverForLocation, the system flag stating that the app does not obtain or infer your location from nearby Bluetooth devices. The app does not read your position, does not store it and does not send it. On Android 12 and later the permission is not even requested, because the system no longer requires it for scanning.

4. When data leaves the phone

There are four cases and no others.

4.1 Downloading the recipe catalogue — automatic, can be turned off

The app requests a public file from GitHub:

https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Jmalpica1010/singularcoffee-app/main/recipes.json

It is a download: it asks for the file and sends nothing. It carries no identifiers, no data of yours, nothing from your database. As with any connection, GitHub sees your IP address simply because your phone connects to its server; GitHub handles that under its own policy, and we neither receive nor log it.

This happens when you open the app and when you tap "refresh". It is turned off under Settings → Community recipes; it ships enabled. With syncing off the app makes no network connection at all, and still works with the catalogue bundled inside it.

4.2 Contributing a recipe — only if you ask for it

If you tap "contribute recipe", the app opens your browser on a GitHub issue form with the recipe you chose already written out: name, author, dose, ratio, temperature and the steps. Nothing else: not your database, not your coffees, not your tasting notes.

It opens in the browser and does not send itself: you see exactly what it says before publishing it, and you can close it. A GitHub issue is public, and you publish it with your GitHub account, not with the app.

4.3 Reporting a problem — only if you ask for it

Under Settings → Report a problem a text report is generated and Android's share sheet opens so you can send it wherever you want. It contains:

  • App version, Android version, phone manufacturer and model, language.
  • The scale's advertised name, protocol, connection state, last reading, flow rate and battery.
  • The last twelve frames received from the scale, in hexadecimal. These are raw weights and they exist to diagnose a wrong reading, which cannot be diagnosed by looking at the weight. They live only in memory and are lost when the app closes.
  • The numbers from your last brew: dose, water, time, grind, temperature, pours, TDS, dry spent grounds and the recipe name.

It deliberately leaves out the coffee's name, the roaster and your tasting notes: that is your content and it does not help fix anything.

You choose who you send it to, and you can read and edit it before sending.

4.4 Android backup — automatic, belongs to the system, can be turned off

The app allows Android's automatic backup and includes the app's database (singular_coffee.db) in it, so that changing phones or reinstalling does not lose your history.

This means your brews, coffees, equipment and tasting notes are copied to your Google Drive account, into the app-backup space. It is an operating-system service: Android makes the copy, not the app, it is encrypted, and only you can restore it. Google handles it under its privacy policy.

You turn it off from the system, under Android Settings → Google → Backup, or by removing this app from the backup. If you do, your data stays on the phone only and a reinstall starts from scratch.

5. Label text recognition

Label scanning uses Google's ML Kit, in the variant distributed by Google Play Services (play-services-mlkit-text-recognition). Google Play installs the model and recognition runs on the phone.

  • The photograph is not sent to any server, neither Google's nor ours.
  • The recognised text does not leave the phone either.
  • Google may receive information about downloading and installing the module, as with any Play Services component, under its policy.

6. The share image

When you finish a brew you can generate an image with its data. The app writes it to the private cache, or to your gallery if you choose to save it, and uses Android's share sheet so you can send it wherever you want.

The image contains what is visible on it: the coffee's name and roaster, origin, process, dose, water, ratio, time, temperature, grind, extraction yield, the recipe with its author, your curve, your cupping radar, your descriptors, your score and your Instagram username if you set one. If you publish it, all of that becomes public — the handle is there for exactly that reason. The app does not send it anywhere on its own.

7. Who your data is shared with

Nobody. It is not sold, rented or handed over, and it is not used for advertising or for training models. There is no server for it to be sent to.

The only third parties involved are the ones above, and only as described: GitHub (when downloading the catalogue, or if you decide to publish a recipe), Google (Play Services for the text-recognition module, and Android backup if you have it enabled) and whichever app you choose when sharing an image or a report.

8. How long data is kept, and how to delete it

It is kept as long as you want: there is no expiry and no automatic deletion.

  • A single record: delete it from within the app.
  • Everything: uninstall the app, or use Android Settings → Apps → SingularCoffee → Storage → Clear data. Since everything is local, no copy remains anywhere else.
  • If you had Android backup enabled, delete that copy from your Google account too: uninstalling the app does not delete a backup the system already made.

You do not need to ask us to delete anything, because we hold nothing of yours. For the same reason, we cannot recover lost data.

9. Security

The database lives in the app's private storage, which other apps cannot reach on an unmodified Android. Temporary photographs go to the private cache. The only network connection is an HTTPS download.

The app stores no passwords, keys or payment details, because it does not ask for any.

10. Children

The app is not directed at children under 13, collects identity data from nobody, and does no profiling. It has no inappropriate content and no internal social features.

11. International transfers

We do not transfer your data to any country, because we do not receive it. The connections in section 4 go to GitHub and Google servers, which may sit outside your country and are governed by their own policies.

12. Your rights

Because no personal data is collected, there is no file of yours to request access to, or to rectify, erase, object to or port: the data is on your phone and you control it directly, including exporting it as an image or deleting it entirely.

If you live in the European Union, the United Kingdom, California or Mexico and believe something in this policy is not being honoured, write to malpica.jonatan@gmail.com.

13. Changes to this policy

If it changes, the date in the header is updated and the full history remains in the public repository. A change that widens what leaves the phone will be announced inside the app before it happens, not after.
